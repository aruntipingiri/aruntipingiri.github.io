About Me

I am a Machine Learning and Computer Vision researcher and recent M.S. graduate from Georgia Tech (Spring 2026), currently preparing for Ph.D. study. My research investigates self-supervised representation learning, open-set visual retrieval, and zero-shot clustering in vision foundation models under severe, fine-grained domain shifts.

In a joint collaboration with the Florida Museum of Natural History (Prof. Arthur Porto / Moritz Lurig), my work demonstrated that self-supervised vision transformers (DINOv3) autonomously recover fine-grained species boundaries without taxonomic supervision (ECCV 2026 CVNH Oral). My technical approach pairs foundation model representations with non-Euclidean manifold learning, graph modularity, and Bayesian probabilistic inference. Prior to focusing on deep learning research, I established a comprehensive engineering background designing resilient, high-throughput distributed data architectures.

Publications & Research

Self-Supervised Vision Embeddings Reveal Phenotype-Taxonomy Structure in Digitized Butterfly Collections Oral Presentation
Arun Tipingiri, Arthur Porto
European Conference on Computer Vision (ECCV 2026) – Computer Vision for Natural Heritage (CVNH) Workshop / Springer
Developed a zero-shot, taxonomy-free visual clustering and collection triage framework for large-scale natural-history archives using self-supervised Vision Transformers (DINOv3 ViT-L/16). Evaluated across 10 focal butterfly genera (741 species, 11,821 images) from an archive of 189k+ specimens, demonstrating that self-supervised embeddings autonomously recover species-level boundaries and isolate phenotypic modes without manual annotations. Conducted via Georgia Tech CS 8903 / 6999 research credits in collaboration with the Florida Museum of Natural History.

Research Interests

  • Generalized Category Discovery & Open-Set Retrieval: Metric learning, manifold learning, and dynamic parameter-space partitioning to identify and cluster novel visual classes without catastrophic forgetting.
  • Multimodal Foundation Model Verification: Mitigating agreement bias and perceptual hallucinations in vision-language models when evaluating fine-grained, open-world sensory data.
  • Geometric & Graph-Structured Representations: Intersecting continuous representation learning with discrete graph topologies and non-Euclidean geometries for structured visual reasoning.
